"Too slow, too sluggish, too cautious". Why German automotive suppliers are increasingly bringing their innovations to C

"Too slow, too sluggish, too cautious". Why German automotive suppliers are increasingly bringing their innovations to Chinese carmakers first. For many years there was an almost symbiotic relationship between German automakers and their suppliers. Innovations such as electronic fuel injection, ABS and ESP or airbag systems were often invented, but always industrialised and brought to success together. In today's edition, the Handelsblatt looks at how the elite of German automotive suppliers, under the impression of the challenges facing European and especially German manufacturers, are increasingly turning to producing the latest innovations with the up-and-coming Chinese brands. It was a pleasure for me to contribute to this article with an analysis of the Chinese supplier industry. You will also find quotes from Arnd Franz (Mahle), Stefan Hartung (BOSCH), Harald Marquardt (Marquardt Group) and Stephan von Schuckmann (ZF), Harald Marquardt, CEO of the group of the same name, says: "With a Chinese electric car costing around €100,000, you will hardly recognise that it is a non-European vehicle". Would you like some examples? ZF: The first steer-by-wire went to NIO, 800V power electronics can be found in several Chinese vehicles. BOSCH: infotainment controller to Changan Conti: break-by-wire to Leapmotor Perhaps the best example of the new China strategy comes from ZF: Stephan von Schuckmann, member of the board and head of the driveline division, will in future manage his global unit from Shanghai. "The build quality is very high, the software expertise extraordinary. These are very, very good vehicles and they are on the road in a third of the time," says von Schuckmann. Even though China itself is facing some major challenges and international cooperation with and through the People's Republic will be rewritten in the coming years, one thing is certain for German suppliers - as the Handelsblatt puts it: “they will not go down with the German automotive industry”. The German manufacturers' electric offensive had a late start, and it is precisely the short-term effects that are preventing sales from growing to the desired extent. In markets such as Germany, they are even facing a decline in sales. They are trying to counteract this with innovative cooperation models in China (such as AUDI's acquisition of a SAIC electric platform to complement its own MEB platform). While German carmakers are losing market share due to the strengthening of Chinese manufacturers, this development can be an opportunity for suppliers to reduce their dependence on domestic manufacturers. "Intel CEO Pat Gelsinger is counting on Europe, of all places, to return to the top of the #semiconductorindustry", just

"Intel CEO Pat Gelsinger is counting on Europe, of all places, to return to the top of the #semiconductorindustry", just reports Handelsblatt. It was a pleasure for me to contribute to this piece with some technological assessments. * In the course of its IDM 2.0 Strategy (Integrated Device Manufacturing) - presented last year - Intel Corporation wants to become (again) one of the leading foundry services providers worldwide. * In addition to new plants at the Ocotillo/Arizona site, an expansion of capacities in Europe is also planned. * Intel wants to build two huge factories and several research and development sites in the EU with massive state support. More than 20 billion euros are to be invested for this purpose. * For example, new, technologically leading factories are to be built in Magdeburg and Italy. It also wants to expand in France, Spain and Poland with state support. In this way, an expected funding sum of around ten billion euros should come together. * This is a great opportunity for Europe, as it would be a chance to bring a factory for "2nm technology" (or now more nicely called technology of "the Ångström-era") to the continent. * Pat Gelsinger, an engineer and inveterate geek, as he calls himself, wants to make Intel the first chip producer in the world to use the new lithography systems for high-NA-EUV (Twinscan EXE:5x00-series, high numerical aperture) from the Dutch equipment manufacturer ASML in series production. * Due to the significantly improved resolution of these stepper machines, the finest chip structures under 3nm can be produced much more efficiently than is possible at all with today's equipment. * The technology is planned to be used for the first time in the "Intel 18A" production process from the end of 2025. With the help of these technologies, Gelsinger wants to replace the Taiwanese contract manufacturer TSMC as the technology leader. Read more about this topic in the Handelsblatt article online (link in the commentary) or in the print edition. The Silent Revolution of E-Motors: DER SPIEGEL sheds light on an often overlooked key component of electric vehicles

🔌 The Silent Revolution of E-Motors: DER SPIEGEL sheds light on an often overlooked key component of electric vehicles For years, the public discourse around electric vehicles has focused almost exclusively on the battery—its chemistry, energy density, raw materials, and charging speed. But quietly and steadily, a different core component is evolving at remarkable speed: the electric motor. I recently had the privilege to contribute to a detailed feature in Der Spiegel that shines a long-overdue spotlight on how electrical drive systems are advancing. The article captures a wave of innovation happening across OEMs, startups, and research institutions that promises to make EVs more efficient, more affordable, and more sustainable—well beyond the battery pack. As I noted in the article: “In der öffentlichen Diskussion dreht sich beim Elektroauto fast alles um die Batterie. Dabei steckt auch in der E-Maschine noch enormes Potenzial, um Elektrofahrzeuge effizienter, leistungsstärker, leichter, umweltfreundlicher und günstiger zu machen.” And it’s not just the e-motor in isolation: Efficiency is a team sport. It takes the entire propulsion chain—from battery and inverter to the electric motor, drive mechanics, tyres, and even the vehicle’s software and thermal management—to unlock true, system-level performance. Only when all these components are finely tuned and orchestrated can we reach the next level of energy efficiency and driving experience. And this end-to-end approach is where our teams at Capgemini shine. The innovations emerging from Europe are impressive: 🔹 Mercedes-Benz is pioneering ultra-efficient motors with its “gesehnte Spulen” and double V-shaped magnets, achieving a 93% long-distance efficiency—positioning the upcoming CLA as the “one-liter car” of the electric era, as CEO Ola Källenius put it. 🔹 BMW (for the "Neue Klasse") is developing synchronous motors with no rare earths, and cutting energy loss by 40% and weight by 10%. 🔹 RWTH Aachen University (a cooperation partner of ours) and Hyperdrives are working on the industrialization of internally cooled copper hairpins—a breakthrough likened to a turbocharger for EVs as said by Till Augustin Backes. 🔹 AEM Advanced Electric Machines is eliminating both rare earths and copper, using aluminum windings for better recyclability and cost efficiency, as noted by CEO James Widmer. 🔹 DeepDrive is introducing a double-rotor radial flux motor, while YASA (a Mercedes subsidiary) is scaling axial flux technology to shrink motors dramatically. 👉 Read the full Der Spiegel feature here: https://lnkd.in/duzghj-i #ElectricVehicles #Innovation #Automotive #SustainableMobility Werner Ferreira da Silva Michael Müller Baligh Belgacem Christoph Weissinger Marcus Fiege Fabienne LEFEVER Jens Sulek Jochen Bechtold Capgemini Automotive
